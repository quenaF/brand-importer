# Stakeholders

## Developer or AI coding agent

Needs a fast, deterministic path from authorized source material to implementation-ready outputs. Risks include hidden assumptions, inconsistent exporters, and overconfident automation.

## Brand owner

Needs recognizable representation, correction authority, and understandable review. Risks include technical overload, false approval, or seeing a visually close result treated as authentic.

## Product designer

Needs a portable system with traceable decisions, not a pile of scraped values. Risks include inheriting noise, inaccessible combinations, and unexplained role assignments.

## Marketing or communications lead

Needs language and imagery guidance to preserve positioning across the white-label product. Risks include flattening the voice into generic adjectives or quoting outdated copy as current strategy.

## QA and accessibility reviewers

Need deterministic fixtures, contrast checks, asset integrity checks, and explicit unresolved states. Risks include a schema-valid pack that still produces unusable or inaccessible interfaces.

## Product operator

Needs safe updates when a tenant changes branding. Risks include silent drift, unreviewed replacements, and breaking every tenant when one adapter changes.

## End user

Needs a coherent, trustworthy, accessible experience. They should not have to detect whether a brand import was inaccurate or whether the product is pretending to be official.

## Maintainers of Brand Importer

Need clean boundaries between extraction, interpretation, owner review, the canonical Brand Pack, and exporters. Risks include framework-specific logic leaking into the core contract.

## Stakeholder priority rule

When priorities conflict, protect authorization, truthfulness, accessibility, and owner correction before speed or visual polish.

## Evidence status

This stakeholder map is **Observed—plan** and should be revised when real users expose missing roles or consequences.
